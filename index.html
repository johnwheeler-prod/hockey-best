Criterion 2

Physicality of Play

  • NHL: ~40–50+ combined hits per game
  • NHL hits average 35g peak acceleration vs. NFL's 29.2g
  • That's ~17% harder — despite NHL players being nearly 20% smaller
  • Physics: skating velocity at contact exceeds running velocity
  • NBA / MLB: no meaningful full-body contact as a core game mechanic

* The Sports Science methodology has critics. Noted. Moving on.

Criterion 3

Hardest Skills Required

ESPN rated 60 sports across 10 athletic skill categories.

#2 of 60

Ice Hockey — behind only boxing. Ahead of football, basketball, and baseball.

But wait — hitting a baseball is the hardest single skill in sports.
95mph fastball. 400ms to the plate. 150ms to decide. Half-inch margin of error.
Michael Jordan couldn't do it.

Baseball has one hard skill.
Hockey requires you to skate, stickhandle, pass, and shoot — simultaneously — while someone hits you at 35g.
The ESPN panel agreed.

Criterion 4

TV Ratings Trajectory

NHL ↑

  • 2025–26: up ~40% YoY — fastest growth of any major sport
  • 2024 SCF Game 7: 7.7M viewers, peaked at 10.3M
  • 2024 playoffs: up 60% vs. prior year

NBA ↓

  • Down ~50% since 2011–12
  • 2024–25: down another 2%, early-season drops as steep as 20%
  • Load management. Stars sitting out national TV games.

NFL: still dominant. This is the one honest concession.
MLB: not growing. Moving on.

Criterion 5, for contrast

Jay Cutler — 2011 NFC Championship

Bears vs. Packers. Super Bowl berth on the line.

Cutler suffered a Grade II MCL sprain. Attempted one pass in the third quarter. His knee buckled. He was done.

The reaction, from fellow NFL players, in real time:

"All I'm saying is that he can finish the game on a hurt knee... I played the whole season on one." — Maurice Jones-Drew, Jacksonville Jaguars RB
"If he was my teammate I would be looking at him sideways." — Asante Samuel, Eagles CB

A Grade II MCL sprain heals on its own in 3–4 weeks. No surgery required.
His own coach confirmed: the trainers made the call. It wasn't Cutler's choice.

In the NHL, that's called Tuesday.

But also

The Honor Code

You've seen what they play through.
Now let's talk about who they are.

Game 6, 2016 Stanley Cup Final — Sidney Crosby, mic'd up
Malkin feeds Kunitz a wide-open net. Kunitz passes back. Doesn't score. Bench starts grumbling.
"Hey, guys — I think he knows. Like, go give him a f*cking tap. It's okay. We'll get another f*cking chance." — Crosby, to the bench. Then skated directly to Kunitz: "Don't worry about it. Keep going, you'll get another one. Let's go!"

The "it started in our own zone" post-game deflection is so universal in hockey it's been satirized — viral compilations of players using the exact same phrases interchangeably. In other sports, stars take credit. In hockey, the culture enforces the opposite.

The Honor Code, continued

And then there's what they do off the ice.

  • Hockey Fights Cancer: $8.2M raised in 2024–25 (a record). $44M+ all-time across 26 years. 25 research grants awarded.
  • NHL + NHLPA combined: $14M+ invested in communities in 2023–24 alone.
  • NHLPA Goals & Dreams: $27M+ in equipment donated to grassroots hockey since 1999.
  • NHL Industry Growth Fund: $180M+ invested over its first decade.
  • Hockey is for Everyone: 120,000+ children given access to the sport since 1998.

Okay, for real though.

Why Hockey is Actually Special

  • The speed — no other team sport moves this fast
  • The physicality and the skill, coexisting — it is both the hardest-hitting and the most technically demanding
  • The culture — the handshake line after a playoff series. Players who spent years hating each other, shaking hands and meaning it.
  • The playoffs — nothing in sports matches the intensity of a Game 7 in overtime
